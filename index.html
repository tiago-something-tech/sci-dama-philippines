THE BASICS

How to play

Red starts. Play another person in PvP, the computer in PvAI, or both sides in Playground. The lower final score wins.

In PvAI, choose First (Red) or Second (Blue). Red always opens; if you choose Blue, the computer moves first. The board stays facing your side. Dumbledore deliberately chooses poor moves; Joe aims to look two moves ahead for each side; Magnus searches deeper within a thinking-time limit. Magnus is our strongest level, not guaranteed unbeatable. PvAI is untimed. Undo takes back your move and the computer’s reply.

  1. Move diagonally. Ordinary chips move one square forward and capture forward or backward. A dama travels along an open diagonal.
  2. Captures are compulsory. If a dama can capture, it takes priority over every ordinary chip. Within the same class, you must take the path capturing the most pieces.
  3. Score on landing. Apply the landing square’s operation to the capturing chip’s value first and the captured chip’s value second. Each jump is scored separately using the chip’s original value.
  4. Dama changes the score. Multiply a capture result by 2 if either chip is dama, or by 4 if both are dama.
  5. Finish and compare. Add the values of each player’s remaining chips to their running total. Lower wins. A move without a capture adds no points.
Practice-version decisions

PvP flips the board after each completed turn. Playground lets you play both sides with manual board flipping. The optional PvP timer allows one minute for the entire turn, including multiple captures. Returning to the main menu pauses the timer. When time runs out, play pauses for player review; no winner is assigned automatically. Promotion occurs after the complete turn. Captured pieces are removed immediately. Equal-class, equal-count paths are both allowed. Remaining dama values are counted once. These edge cases need your review before tournament use.

Repetition does not end a game automatically. Use “Finish & score” when both players agree. Having no legal move ends play and compares final scores.

Calculations and comparisons use exact fractions internally. The display shows up to 15 significant digits; ≈ marks a shortened result. Open a move’s calculation to see its exact value.
